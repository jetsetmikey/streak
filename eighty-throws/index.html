Day 1: leave London

80 Throws

Throw a paper plane once a day. Get it all the way around the world before Phileas Fogg does it in 80 days.

  • Drag on the map toward where you want to go. Let go to throw.
  • The wind pushes your plane. At London's latitude it mostly blows east, so ride it.
  • Land in a new country to collect its stamp. Land in the sea and your plane gets soggy.
  • Fogg never skips a day. Neither should you.

Passport

How it works

  • One throw a day. Drag on the map to aim, and drag farther to throw harder. Let go to throw.
  • Throwing too hard can stall. The preview turns amber, then red, when there's a risk.
  • The wind is modelled on Earth's real wind belts. Westerlies blow east between about 30° and 60° north and south. Trade winds blow west near the equator. Each day brings new weather on top.
  • Sea landings make your plane soggy. Your next throw flies 30% shorter.
  • The race. Cross every line of longitude to get home. Fogg keeps to his 1872 timetable (Suez, Bombay, Hong Kong, San Francisco, New York) and gets back to London on day 80.
